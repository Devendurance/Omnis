# useOmnis Brand Messaging

**Master brand:** useOmnis  
**Spoken short form:** Omnis  
**Category:** Bounded financial execution agent for stablecoin payments and machine commerce  
**Descriptor:** Bounded financial execution for humans and agents.  
**Tagline:** **Tell Omnis what needs to get paid. It handles the rest.**

This document turns the approved seven-part useOmnis scope into a repeatable verbal system for the website, product, demo, pitch deck, service integrations, and partner conversations.

---

## 1. Brand Core and Naming Architecture

### What useOmnis is

useOmnis turns financial intent into controlled, completed, and provable action.

A user gives Omnis:

- a task;
- a budget;
- rules;
- allowed capabilities;
- approval thresholds.

Omnis coordinates the services, payments, approvals, settlement, and proof required to complete the task without giving an agent unrestricted authority over the user's money.

### The idea to remember

> **Delegation without surrendering control.**

### Master brand usage

Use `useOmnis` in the formal product name, URL, repository, and first mention. Use `Omnis` in ordinary product copy after the first mention.

Correct:

> useOmnis is a bounded financial execution agent. Tell Omnis what needs to get paid.

Avoid:

- `UseOmnis`
- `use omnis`
- `Omnis AI`
- `Omnis Wallet`
- `Omnis Finance`

### Product modes

- **Omnis Pay**: direct financial actions such as contractor payments, invoice settlement, and stablecoin transfers.
- **Omnis Delegate**: bounded agent tasks where the agent may purchase services or data to complete a larger job.

### Supporting modules

- **Omnis Intent**: turns natural-language requests into structured tasks.
- **Omnis Policy**: defines budgets, rules, allowed capabilities, recipients, assets, and approval thresholds.
- **Omnis Services**: discovers and compares paid machine services.
- **Omnis Approval**: surfaces the human decision boundary.
- **Omnis Settlement**: completes the final payment.
- **Omnis Proof**: creates the unified execution record.

Do not turn Arc, Circle, Hedera, Privy, x402, wallets, bridges, or chains into competing product brands. They are infrastructure layers that enable Omnis.

## 2. Strategic Foundation and Positioning

### Purpose

Make financial delegation usable without making control invisible.

### Vision

A world where people and software agents can complete financial work across services and networks while the user's rules, not an opaque model, define what may happen.

### Mission

Turn financial intent into a structured, controlled, completed, and provable action.

### Primary audience

Crypto-native individuals and small teams who already hold or receive stablecoins, use AI to get work done, and increasingly need payments to happen inside those workflows.

This includes:

- solo builders;
- small startups;
- DAO/core contributors;
- researchers;
- creators;
- operators;
- developer teams.

### Product wedge

**Internal phrase:** controlled economic delegation.  
**External phrase:** bounded financial agency.

### Category

> **Bounded financial execution agent for stablecoin payments and machine commerce.**

This is clearer than leading with “agentic payments,” “AI wallet,” or “x402 marketplace.”

### Core problem

A simple financial task can require wallet inspection, service discovery, paid data, network decisions, approvals, settlement, and proof. Existing AI assistants understand language but do not provide deterministic financial control. Wallets submit transactions but do not understand the complete task.

useOmnis connects the two.

### Promise

> **Tell Omnis what needs to get paid, what it may spend, and what rules apply. It coordinates the work, asks when approval is needed, completes the payment, and returns the proof.**

### Difference

useOmnis combines:

- natural-language financial intent;
- structured task extraction;
- explicit budget and policy enforcement;
- paid service discovery;
- machine-to-machine payments;
- human approval escalation;
- stablecoin settlement;
- one unified proof object.

The distinctive difference is not simply that an agent can pay. It is that the agent can pay **for a reason, within a boundary, with an auditable result**.

### Emotional payoff

> “I told Omnis what I wanted, saw what it planned to do, it stayed within my rules, and now I have proof.”

### Strategic enemy

**Unbounded delegation:** AI agents with unrestricted wallet access, unexplained service charges, opaque tool calls, and financial actions that cannot be reconstructed afterward.

### Positioning statement

> For crypto-native individuals and small teams who use stablecoins and AI to get work done, useOmnis is a bounded financial execution agent that turns plain-language tasks into controlled service purchases, approvals, stablecoin settlement, and proof. Unlike a generic AI assistant or a standalone wallet, useOmnis separates interpretation from financial authority and makes every meaningful spend part of a visible policy path.

### Brand principles

1. Outcome before infrastructure.
2. Bounded authority.
3. Every spend has a reason.
4. Approval is a product state.
5. Proof closes the loop.
6. Cross-chain stays quiet unless inspected.
7. Failure and recovery are part of the product.

> **Tell Omnis what needs to get paid. It handles the rest.**

> Give an AI agent a financial task, a spending limit, and clear rules. Omnis can buy the services it needs, ask before crossing a boundary, settle the payment, and return one proof record.

### Short pitch

> Omnis turns financial intent into controlled execution. You describe the outcome, set the budget and rules, and the agent coordinates services, approvals, settlement, and proof without receiving unrestricted authority.

### Thirty-second pitch

Paying someone in crypto can require wallet checks, service accounts, network decisions, approvals, settlement, and proof. useOmnis lets you describe the outcome instead. Tell Omnis to pay a contractor, check the wallet first, and spend no more than five cents on the check. It turns that request into a structured plan, buys the required service within the budget, asks for approval when necessary, settles the payment, and returns a complete record of what happened.

### Brand statement

useOmnis gives people a way to delegate financial work without turning trust into a blank cheque. The user defines the task, budget, rules, and approval boundary. Omnis coordinates the services and settlement, then returns a readable record of the work.

### Messaging house

#### Roof: financial intent becomes completed action

> **From “How do I coordinate this?” to “I need this done, under these rules.”**

#### Pillar 1: Turn intent into a plan

**Message:** Say what needs to happen. Omnis turns it into a structured task.

**Proof points:**

- Recipient, amount, purpose, budget, and constraints become visible fields.
- Missing information is surfaced before money moves.
- The user can review the intended action before execution.
- Conversational interpretation is separated from transaction submission.

**Customer outcome:** Less time translating a real-world job into wallet operations.

#### Pillar 2: Let the agent spend inside a boundary

**Message:** An agent can buy what it needs without receiving unlimited authority.

**Proof points:**

- Per-task budgets.
- Per-service limits.
- Allowed capabilities.
- Approval thresholds.
- Spend tracking.
- Deterministic policy enforcement.

**Customer outcome:** Delegation without turning the wallet into a blank cheque.

#### Pillar 3: Finish with settlement and proof

**Message:** A financial task is not finished until the payment is settled and explainable.

**Proof points:**

- Stablecoin settlement.
- Approval history.
- Service receipts.
- Transaction state.
- Final proof bundle.
- Recovery states when something fails.

**Customer outcome:** Confidence that the job was completed and can be verified afterward.

## 4. Feature-to-Value Translations

### Natural-language task input

- **Feature:** The user describes a financial outcome conversationally.
- **Functional result:** Omnis creates a structured task.
- **Practical outcome:** The user does not manually coordinate every primitive action.
- **Emotional meaning:** “I can state the job without becoming the workflow engine.”

### Structured Task Brief

- **Feature:** Recipient, amount, asset, purpose, constraints, and budget are extracted.
- **Functional result:** The proposed action becomes inspectable.
- **Practical outcome:** Ambiguity can be corrected before spending.
- **Emotional meaning:** “The agent understood what I meant.”

### Policy and budget enforcement

- **Feature:** Task budgets, service budgets, allowed capabilities, and approval thresholds.
- **Functional result:** Agent authority is bounded by explicit rules.
- **Practical outcome:** The agent can act without having unrestricted access.
- **Emotional meaning:** “I delegated the work, not control of everything.”

### Paid service discovery

- **Feature:** Omnis finds and compares machine services.
- **Functional result:** The agent can purchase the capability needed to complete a task.
- **Practical outcome:** No manual service-account or credit-card detour for every small capability.
- **Emotional meaning:** “The agent can be resourceful without being reckless.”

### Human approval boundary

- **Feature:** The agent asks before crossing a defined threshold.
- **Functional result:** Irreversible or higher-value actions receive explicit approval.
- **Practical outcome:** Automation continues until human judgment is actually needed.
- **Emotional meaning:** “Approval is there when it matters, not every time.”

### Stablecoin settlement

- **Feature:** The final payment is executed through the supported settlement path.
- **Functional result:** The intended financial action reaches completion.
- **Practical outcome:** Service activity and final payment live in one task record.
- **Emotional meaning:** “The work ended in a real result.”

### Proof Bundle

- **Feature:** One record connects intent, policy, service activity, approval, settlement, and evidence.
- **Functional result:** The user can reconstruct what happened.
- **Practical outcome:** Easier review, support, accounting, and trust.
- **Emotional meaning:** “I do not have to remember or hunt for the story.”

## 5. Verbal Identity

### Personality

- **Capable:** knows how to coordinate difficult work.
- **Bounded:** never presents authority as unlimited.
- **Clear:** explains the plan in ordinary language.
- **Accountable:** shows what was bought, approved, settled, and recorded.
- **Calm:** never feels like a casino, trading terminal, or breathless AI demo.
- **Practical:** focused on completing the job.

### Voice rules

#### 1. Lead with the outcome

- **Use:** “Pay the contractor after checking the wallet.”
- **Avoid:** “Orchestrate a multi-chain x402-enabled settlement workflow.”

#### 2. Make the budget visible

- **Use:** “Wallet check budget: $0.05. Contractor payment: $50.00.”
- **Avoid:** “The agent has limited spending authority.”

#### 3. Explain what the agent is doing

- **Use:** “Omnis is comparing two wallet-check services.”
- **Avoid:** “Omnis is thinking.”

#### 4. Treat approval as normal

- **Use:** “Approval needed: this payment is above your automatic limit.”
- **Avoid:** “The agent failed to complete the task.”

#### 5. Separate recommendation from execution

- **Use:** “Omnis recommends Service A. It costs $0.03. Purchase this service?”
- **Avoid:** Combining recommendation, payment, and execution into one unexplained action.

#### 6. Make failure actionable

- **Use:** “The service did not respond. No payment was made. Try the second approved provider?”
- **Avoid:** “Something went wrong.”

### Cadence

- Short sentences.
- Concrete verbs.
- One decision per line.
- Plain-language summary first.
- Technical details second.
- No artificial AI filler.
- No excessive exclamation marks.

### Vocabulary to use

`task`, `plan`, `policy`, `rule`, `budget`, `allowance`, `service`, `capability`, `approval`, `settlement`, `receipt`, `proof`, `completed`, `blocked`, `waiting`, `retry`, `no payment made`, `within your limit`.

### Vocabulary to avoid

`magic`, `omnipotent`, `autonomous wealth`, `unstoppable`, `frictionless`, `trustless`, `genius`, `hallucination-proof`, `guaranteed`, `risk-free`, `human-free`, `future of payments`, and `AI-powered` unless the specific AI behaviour is being described.

### Before and after

**Before:**

> Our autonomous AI financial agent seamlessly orchestrates cross-chain machine commerce.

**After:**

> Tell Omnis what needs to happen. It shows the plan, spends within your rules, asks before crossing a limit, and returns the proof.

### Contextual tone

- **Task creation:** calm and direct.
- **Plan review:** precise and explanatory.
- **Approval:** serious, concise, impossible to misread.
- **Service discovery:** comparative and transparent.
- **Failure:** accountable and recovery-oriented.
- **Proof:** factual, complete, and restrained.
- **Hackathon demo:** confident but not theatrical.

## 6. Product Language and Surface Copy

- `tasks`
- `policies`
- `services`
- `approvals`
- `proof`
- `start a task`

### Command input

Placeholder:

> `tell omnis what needs to get paid...`

### Task Brief

Heading:

> `i understand the task`

Example:

> Check the contractor wallet, then pay 50 USDC if the result meets your policy.  
> Wallet-check budget: 0.05 USDC.

### Policy summary

> `this is what omnis may do`

- `contractor payment: 50 usdc`
- `wallet-check budget: 0.05 usdc`
- `automatic service limit: 0.05 usdc`
- `approval required before contractor payment`

### Service discovery

> `omnis found two approved services`

> `lowest-cost option: 0.03 usdc`

Action:

> `use this service`

### Budget ledger

- `allocated`
- `spent`
- `remaining`
- `reason for spend`

Example:

> `service payment: 0.03 usdc`  
> `budget remaining: 0.02 usdc`  
> `reason: contractor wallet verification`

### Approval state

> `approval needed`

> The contractor payment is ready. Confirm 50 USDC to `0x…91F2`.

Actions:

- `approve payment`
- `edit task`
- `cancel`

### Settlement states

- `payment ready`
- `payment settling`
- `settled`
- `settlement blocked`

### Proof state

> `task complete. proof is ready.`

The Proof Bundle should contain:

- original task;
- structured plan;
- policy summary;
- service purchase;
- service result;
- human approval;
- settlement transaction;
- timestamp;
- final status.

### Error and recovery states

> `service unavailable. no payment was made. try the second approved provider?`

> `approval expired. no settlement was submitted. review the task to continue.`

> `settlement blocked. the policy is unchanged. choose a supported route or edit the task.`

### Empty state

> `no tasks yet`  
> Tell Omnis what needs to get paid. It will show the plan before it spends.  
> `start a task`

## 7. Visual and Interaction Messaging

### Recommended direction

> **useOmnis as the master brand, Omnis Pay and Omnis Delegate as the two product modes, and Mandate to Proof as the visual and interaction territory.**

### Visual idea in words

A cosmic field represents the range of work an agent can coordinate. A luminous Mandate Line gives that possibility structure by moving through intent, policy, service, approval, settlement, and proof.

The brand should feel expansive in atmosphere but disciplined in action.

### What the identity must communicate

- The product is bigger than a wallet.
- The product is more accountable than a chatbot.
- The agent can act, but not without limits.
- Services can be purchased, but every spend has a reason.
- Approval is deliberate, not a failure.
- The final output is a completed task and a proof record.

### Do not let the visual system imply

- unrestricted autonomy;
- guaranteed payment success;
- risk-free finance;
- anonymous or invisible money movement;
- speculative trading;
- a generic AI assistant;
- a chain-first product.

## 8. Hackathon and Demo Messaging

### Central story

> **Agents can understand financial tasks. useOmnis gives them bounded authority to complete those tasks, and proves what happened.**

### Flagship task

> “Pay this contractor 50 USDC, but check the wallet first. Spend no more than $0.05 checking.”

### 90-second demo script

**0–10 seconds, Problem**

> “Paying a contractor in crypto can require wallet checks, service accounts, network decisions, approvals, settlement, and proof.”

**10–20 seconds, Intent**

> “I’ll give Omnis the outcome instead.”

Enter the flagship task.

**20–35 seconds, Plan**

Show the Task Brief, payment amount, service budget, and approval threshold.

> “Omnis understands the task and shows me what it may spend.”

**35–50 seconds, Machine commerce**

Show service discovery and the x402-paid wallet-check service.

> “The agent found a service, compared the price, and paid for the check within the delegated budget.”

**50–65 seconds, Approval boundary**

> “The check is complete. The 50 USDC payment is outside the automatic boundary, so Omnis asks me before settling it.”

**65–78 seconds, Settlement**

Approve the payment and show the settlement state.

**78–90 seconds, Proof**

Open the Proof Bundle.

> “The agent did the work, stayed inside the rules, and left one proof record.”

### Sponsor technology framing

- **Privy:** human authentication and wallet/signing layer.
- **Arc and Circle:** financial home, stablecoin balances, and settlement infrastructure.
- **Hedera:** machine-commerce service payments through x402-gated services.

Do not present the demo as three sponsor integrations. Present one Omnis task moving through a controlled route.

### MVP priority

Prove these moments:

1. Natural-language intent becomes a structured Task Brief.
2. The agent buys one required service within a defined budget.
3. A higher-value payment triggers explicit approval.
4. The payment settles.
5. The Proof Bundle connects the complete story.

Defer:

- a full service marketplace;
- arbitrary multi-chain routing UI;
- ENSv2 identity;
- World verification;
- Ledger hardware escalation;
- broad asset support;
- open-ended agent tools;
- complex agent personality.

### Success test

A first-time user should say:

> “I gave the agent a task and a spending limit. It paid for what it needed, asked me before doing anything outside the rules, completed the payment, and gave me proof.”

If the user says:

> “It’s an AI crypto wallet,”

the framing is not clear enough.

- `shows the plan`;

- `asks before crossing the threshold`;
- `purchases a service within the delegated budget`;
- `settles through the supported payment path`;
- `returns a proof record`;

### Do not claim

- `unrestricted autonomy`;
- `fully autonomous finance`;
- `risk-free payments`;
- `guaranteed settlement`;
- `unhackable agents`;
- `perfect wallet verification`;
- `zero-cost machine commerce`;
- `no approval ever`;
- `safe for everyone`.

### Prototype and infrastructure disclosure

Where applicable, label:

- `testnet demo`;
- `prototype`;
- `simulated service result`;
- `supported settlement path`;
- `service availability`;
- `contract and policy limitations`.

Do not let a hackathon prototype sound like a production guarantee.

## 10. Validation and Next Tests

### Confidence

> **High on strategic direction; medium on final naming and legal availability until formal clearance is completed.**

The product strategy is coherent because the user problem, authority model, flagship task, and proof object reinforce one another. Name ownership, domain availability, trademark status, linguistic interpretation, and production compliance remain unverified.

### Test next

> **Five-second comprehension, task-plan comprehension, approval-boundary comprehension, and whether users can accurately explain what Omnis is not.**

### Test 1: Five-second comprehension

Show the hero for five seconds and ask:

1. What is useOmnis?
2. Who is it for?
3. What does it automate?
4. What happens if the agent needs to spend money?

Expected answer:

> A financial execution agent that can complete payments within a budget and asks before crossing a rule.

### Test 2: Task-plan comprehension

Show the Task Brief for ten seconds and ask:

- What is the task?
- What will Omnis spend money on?
- How much may it spend?
- What happens next?

If the user cannot answer, simplify the Task Brief before adding more infrastructure detail.

### Test 3: Approval-boundary comprehension

Show the approval screen and ask:

- What exactly are you approving?
- Why is approval required?
- What has already happened?
- Did any money move before this approval?

The user should not need to inspect a transaction hash to answer these questions.

### Test 4: Category rejection test

Ask users to describe what useOmnis is not.

The desired answer should include at least two of:

- not merely an AI chatbot;
- not merely a wallet;
- not merely a bridge;
- not merely an x402 marketplace;
- not a chain dashboard;
- not unrestricted autonomous finance.

If users cannot distinguish the product from an AI wallet, the positioning and hero need revision.

Lead with the locked line:

> **useOmnis is a bounded financial execution agent for stablecoin payments and machine commerce.**

Make the central product sequence visible everywhere:

> **Task → Policy → Service → Approval → Settlement → Proof**

That sequence is the product, the demo, the visual system, and the memory structure.
